This week’s Passive House sweep

  • NY heat-pump incentives
  • Wythe Schüco windows
  • Heat pump lab notes
  • Maryland incentives

ExtraReach is still in the planning stage. This week’s sweep (roughly September 21–28) looks at a substantial New York incentive stack, a Wythe–Schüco window partnership, practical large-building heat-pump notes, and Maryland programs that still need a careful reading before they become a project assumption.

New York: a useful incentive comparison for heat pumps

On September 25, the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA) outlined a roughly $135 million New Efficient Homes New York effort for residential new construction. The announcement also describes $75 million in cold-climate air-source heat-pump (ASHP) and ground-source heat-pump (GSHP) incentives, available through December 15, 2029, or until funds run out, plus $25 million for Enhanced Design Support.

The Buildings of Excellence program has awarded more than $12 million to 10 multifamily demonstrations, with a $20 million next round accepting proposals through January 20, 2027. Eight of the ten awarded projects commit to Passive House, and all commit to zero on-site fossil fuels plus solar. New York is not a Virginia program, but it is a useful Mid-Atlantic comparison while Maryland’s federal HOMES/HEAR rebates remain unlaunched on the official page.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

Keep the New York dates and program categories on the comparison sheet: new construction, cold-climate ASHP/GSHP, design support, and demonstration funding. For Virginia planning, treat them as a benchmark—not as money available to a local project.

Wythe + Schüco: a window product watch

A September 23 report says Wythe Windows is partnering with Schüco Polymer to manufacture UPVC windows and doors in the United States. The high-performance tilt-turn line is aimed at airtight Passive House envelopes, where the frame, sash, installation, and continuity of the air-control layer all matter more than a glossy product name.

There is a useful Mid-Atlantic connection here: Baltimore Passive House and related vocational work make the regional installation and training question more concrete. Before I put this product in an ExtraReach envelope shortlist, I want manufacturer-confirmed U-values and current listings from the Passive House Institute (PHI) or Passive House Institute US (Phius). Those details are still TBD, not a reason to assume performance.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

Watch the U-value, spacer, hardware, sill, and installation details together. A domestic high-performance tilt-turn option could help Mid-Atlantic sourcing, but only confirmed data can move it from interesting product news to a design input.

Heat pump lab notes: size the whole building

Passive House Accelerator (PHA) published Jay Fox’s September 25 Session Three of the Heat Pump Solutions Lab. The session maps large-building paths from variable-refrigerant-flow (VRF) systems to packaged terminal heat pumps and wastewater or ground-source systems. It also gets into heat-pump water heater (HPWH) placement and a Hudson Valley school ground-source heat-pump retrofit.

That sequence is a good antidote to choosing equipment from a residential catalog before the building has been understood. The free virtual Heat Pump Solutions Symposium is scheduled for October 23 from noon to 3 p.m. Eastern Time. A lighter calendar note: PHA’s September 30 Prefab Month Spec Session looks at panelized and contemporary approaches, a useful adjacent conversation for a design-friendly Passive House plan.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

Keep the lab’s system range visible: VRF, packaged terminal, wastewater/GSHP, and HPWH placement each create different envelope, distribution, and service decisions. The right answer starts with loads and building use, not with a preferred machine.

Maryland incentives: official pages still call for patience

The Maryland Energy Administration (MEA)’s official Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) rebates page still does not show a launch for the federal Home Owner Managing Energy Savings (HOMES) or Home Electrification and Appliance Rebates (HEAR) programs. That is a useful status check: do not turn an unlaunched federal line into a near-term Maryland homeowner rebate in a cost post.

MEA’s Residential Energy Equity Heat Pump request for information (RFI) closed September 18; the agency notes that late replies may receive less weight. Separately, FY27 Residential Energy Equity grant applications from eligible nonprofits and local governments serving low- and moderate-income households are due October 1, 2026, at 3 p.m. Eastern Time. That grant deadline is live and specific, but it is not the same thing as a launched HOMES or HEAR rebate.

For Virginia and the wider Mid-Atlantic, I’m keeping this distinction in the planning notes: track state and utility opportunities, date every federal status statement, and avoid claiming a Maryland launch until the official MEA page actually says one has happened.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

Maryland has a concrete October 1, 3 p.m. ET equity-grant deadline and a completed heat-pump RFI, while the official IRA page still does not show HOMES/HEAR launch. Keep the framing useful and provisional rather than promising savings that cannot yet be claimed.

What I’m carrying forward

  1. Track NYSERDA program eligibility and dates beside Virginia and utility options.
  2. Request confirmed Wythe–Schüco U-values and PHI/Phius listings before adding the windows to the envelope shortlist.
  3. Use the PHA lab notes to compare VRF, packaged terminal, wastewater/GSHP, and HPWH placement around real loads.
  4. Calendar Maryland’s FY27 Residential Energy Equity deadline for October 1 at 3 p.m. ET, and keep HOMES/HEAR marked unlaunched on the official page.
