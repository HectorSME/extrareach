Weekly November 2, 2026 ~5 min read

This week’s Passive House sweep

  • Phius guidebook v26.1.2 adds METr modeling guidance
  • Phius REVIVE retrofit pilot closes to new projects
  • Phius METr single-family training Nov 9 to 10
  • DC Sustainable Energy Utility opens its income-qualified efficiency fund
  • RMI study finds heat pumps could ease power rates

ExtraReach is still in the planning stage. This week’s sweep (roughly October 26–November 2) is a quieter one. The Passive House Institute US (Phius) put out a guidebook update and has a modeling course coming up. DC opened a new round of efficiency funding for existing affordable buildings, and a new study looks at what heat pumps could mean for electricity rates.

Phius guidebook v26.1.2 adds METr modeling guidance

On October 29, Phius published version 26.1.2 of its Certification Guidebook. Phius describes it as a clarification release: it doesn’t change the certification rules. The main addition is guidance for METr, Phius’s cloud-based energy-modeling tool.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

No change to what a project has to meet. If our energy modeler works in METr, the new guidance is worth a read before modeling starts.

Sources: Phius: What’s new in Guidebook v26.1.2

Phius REVIVE retrofit pilot closes to new projects

The same guidebook release closes Phius’s REVIVE retrofit pilot to new projects. For anyone starting a retrofit now, the guidebook’s current retrofit path is the one to plan around.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

ExtraReach is new construction, so this doesn’t change our plan. Mid-Atlantic retrofit projects, such as rowhouse renovations, should use the guidebook’s current retrofit path rather than REVIVE.

Sources: Phius: What’s new in Guidebook v26.1.2

Phius METr single-family training Nov 9 to 10

Phius is running a live online course on modeling single-family homes in METr on November 9–10. Registration is $750, or $700 for Phius members.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

Useful for designers or consultants we might bring on who haven’t worked in METr yet. It’s online, so there’s no travel.

Sources: Phius event page

DC Sustainable Energy Utility opens its income-qualified efficiency fund

The DC Sustainable Energy Utility (DCSEU) opened its Income Qualified Efficiency Fund for fiscal year 2027 on November 2. The fund pays for heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, air sealing and insulation in affordable multifamily buildings, shelters and clinics. Early-bird applications are due December 18, and the final deadline is January 15.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

This fund covers upgrades to existing buildings, not new Passive House construction. It’s still worth passing along to DC affordable housing owners planning envelope or heat pump work.

Sources: DCSEU Income Qualified Efficiency Fund

RMI study finds heat pumps could ease power rates

This one is background reading. A new study from RMI (formerly Rocky Mountain Institute) finds that in many states, where the grid peaks in summer, there is spare winter capacity for about 86 million more homes to add heat pumps, counting both all-electric and hybrid systems. In those summer-peaking states, RMI estimates that using this headroom could lower residential electricity rates by about 13% on average. RMI describes these as directional estimates, not forecasts.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

No action needed. It supports the case that all-electric homes can help the grid rather than strain it, and a tight Passive House envelope keeps winter heating demand low in the first place.

Sources: RMI report · Canary Media coverage
