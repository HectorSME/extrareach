ExtraReach is still in the planning stage. This week’s sweep (roughly November 16–23) has two material items. New York’s All-Electric Buildings Act is now at the U.S. Supreme Court, and Kirkwood Street Passive House in Dover, Delaware, won a Green Building United Groundbreaker Award. Maryland’s EmPOWER cycle still has no Commission order, and several District of Columbia and utility deadlines remain on the calendar.

Supreme Court dockets a joint cert petition on New York’s All-Electric Buildings Act

A joint certiorari petition (cert for short, a petition asking the Court to hear the case) was filed November 13 and docketed November 17 as No. 26-648. It covers Mulhern Gas v. Mosley and Association of Contracting Plumbers v. City of New York. A response is due December 17.

The New York Department of State still says the fossil-fuel code provisions are suspended and not enforceable. That guidance is still the Department’s July 2 notice.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

This is the nearest large-state all-electric rule for new construction, and it stays paused for months. Watch December 17 and any new Department of State notice.

Sources: Supreme Court docket 26-648 · the petition · New York Department of State notice

Kirkwood Street Passive House in Dover Delaware wins a Green Building United Groundbreaker Award

Kirkwood Street Passive House in Dover, Delaware, won Green Building United’s Groundbreaker Award for Residential & Mixed-Use. The award was presented November 19. The project is four affordable, all-electric single-family homes. All four already hold final Phius CORE Prescriptive 2021 certification, dated June 26, 2026.

The homes are in climate zone 4A, the same zone as much of Virginia. Home Energy Rating System (HERS) scores are 30–33. The house at 235 Kirkwood Street tested at 0.023 cubic feet per minute of air leakage at 50 pascals pressure difference (CFM50) per square foot of enclosure.

The award is recognition for work already certified. It is not a new certification.

Takeaway for ExtraReach

These are finally certified affordable homes in the same climate zone as much of Virginia. The award does not change their certification status.

Sources: Green Building United: 2026 Groundbreaker Award winners · Phius certified project: 235 Kirkwood Street

Still watching

Maryland’s EmPOWER 2027–2029 cycle (Public Service Commission Case 9890) still has no Commission order. The November 18 meeting did not take it up. Utilities filed 2027 surcharge scenarios: Pepco and Delmarva on November 17, and Potomac Edison on November 20. The next Commission meeting is November 24.

The District of Columbia Department of Buildings 2027 construction-code comment period is still not open. Emergency Act A26-0457 remains in effect through January 27, 2027. The DC Sustainable Energy Utility (DCSEU) Income Qualified Efficiency Fund has an early-bird deadline of December 18, and all applications are due January 15.

Pepco Home Performance with ENERGY STAR reservations and completed applications are due December 31. Baltimore Gas and Electric’s (BGE) mail-in heat pump water heater rebate of up to $1,600 also ends December 31.

Sources: Pepco Home Performance with ENERGY STAR · BGE heat pump water heater rebate · DCSEU Income Qualified Efficiency Fund
